# LLMs as Agentic Cooperative Players in Multiplayer UNO

- **Authors**: Yago Romano Martinez, Jesse Roberts
- **arXiv**: [2509.09867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09867)
- **Date**: 2025-09-11
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Citations**: N/A
- **Relevance**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)

## Summary

Tests LLM agents in UNO (turn-based card game) with a twist: the LLM is asked to HELP another player win, not to win itself. Builds a tool integrating decoder-only LLMs into the RLCard environment. Evaluates whether LLMs can act as cooperative agents that understand game state, maintain social goals, and make strategically helpful decisions.

## Key Contributions

- Novel evaluation paradigm: LLM-as-cooperative-helper (not self-interested agent)
- Integration of LLMs into RLCard (open-source card game RL framework)
- Analysis of LLM ability to suppress self-interest for cooperative goals
- Shows that LLMs can act as useful game-playing assistants even without RL fine-tuning

## Connection to Our Work

- **RLCard integration** is directly relevant — we could extend this to GI TCG
- Cooperative AI angle maps to GI TCG's team/co-op modes
- The finding that LLMs can act as strategic assistants (not just players) opens up a new role for LLMs in our pipeline: as deck-building advisors, strategy suggesters, or training signal generators
- The tool for integrating LLMs into RL card game environments could be adapted for GI TCG

## Critical Assessment

**Strengths**: Practical tool (RLCard integration); novel cooperative paradigm; open-source RLCard framework.  
**Weaknesses**: UNO is very simple compared to GI TCG (no deck building, no elemental reactions, no dice); small-scale evaluation; unclear generalization to complex TCGs.
