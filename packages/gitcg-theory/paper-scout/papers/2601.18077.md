## Summary

Benchmarks 17 state-of-the-art LLM agents in 2-5 player Hanabi games. Hanabi is a cooperative card game requiring theory-of-mind reasoning, strategic communication, and reasoning under incomplete information — players see others' cards but not their own. Studies how well LLMs handle cooperative reasoning, implicit communication, and multi-agent coordination.

## Key Contributions

- Largest LLM agent benchmark on cooperative incomplete-information game (17 LLMs, 2-5 players)
- Systematically evaluates theory-of-mind reasoning in LLMs via Hanabi conventions
- Analyzes communication strategies: when LLMs succeed/fail at implicit signaling
- Identifies scaling properties: larger models ≠ better cooperation (diminishing returns)

## Connection to Our Work

- **Cooperative reasoning** in Hanabi mirrors GI TCG's team-based matches and co-op modes
- Theory-of-mind evaluation methodology applicable to opponent modeling in GI TCG
- The finding that larger LLMs don't automatically cooperate better is important for our LLM deck prior design — suggests we need structured reasoning, not just bigger models
- Multi-agent LLM coordination patterns could inform our future multi-agent GI TCG architectures

## Critical Assessment

**Strengths**: Comprehensive benchmark (17 models); novel cooperative reasoning angle; real game environment.  
**Weaknesses**: Hanabi is simpler than GI TCG (no deck building, no resource management, no dice); cs.CL venue may miss game theory depth; unclear if open-source.
