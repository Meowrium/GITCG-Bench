## Summary

Studies a variant of Subtraction Nim where both players have the same move options (impartial rules) but the **ending condition is partizan**: Left wins if remaining tokens are even, Right wins if odd. Computed outcomes for many subtraction sets S and found a surprising phenomenon: **~98% of positions are L-positions** (Left-winning) for large enough n. The main theorem proves that n+1 and n-1 are L-positions when n is an R-position, and that only L-positions can persist indefinitely — explaining why R-positions and other outcome classes are rare.

## Key Contributions

- New variant: partizan ending rule applied to impartial Subtraction Nim
- **Asymmetry dominance result**: L-positions dominate the outcome space (~98% for large n) despite symmetric move options, purely due to partizan ending condition
- Theorem: L-positions propagate forward and backward from R-positions, preventing long chains of non-L positions
- Computational enumeration of outcome classes for many subtraction sets
