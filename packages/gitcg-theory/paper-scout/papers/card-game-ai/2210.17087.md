## Summary

Develops the first AI program (DanZero) for GuanDan, a complex Chinese card game similar to Doudizhu but more complicated (large state/action space, long episodes, variable player count). Uses a distributed training framework: actor processes generate self-play samples, learner process updates via Deep Monte-Carlo. Trained for 30 days on 160 CPUs + 1 GPU. Achieves human-level performance, outperforming 8 heuristic baselines.

## Key Contributions

- **First GuanDan AI**: Tackles a card game more complex than Doudizhu (which was itself considered hard)
- **Distributed self-play at scale**: 160 CPU actors demonstrate practical large-scale RL training for card games
- **Deep Monte-Carlo**: Simple but effective — no bootstrapping, just Monte Carlo returns
- **Human evaluation**: Validated against human players, not just algorithmic baselines

## Connection to Our Work

- **GuanDan vs GI TCG**: Both are complex multi-player card games with large action spaces and long episodes. GuanDan's challenges (variable actions, partial observability) are shared with GI TCG.
- **Distributed training architecture**: The 160-CPU distributed self-play setup is a reference architecture for scaling GI TCG training. Our GI TCG environment could support similar distributed self-play.
- **Deep MC vs MCTS**: DanZero uses Deep MC (no tree search) vs our planned MCTS approach. Comparing these methodologies could inform our algorithm choice.
- **State feature design**: Their careful state feature engineering for a complex card game is instructive for GI TCG state representation.

## Critical Assessment

**Strengths**: Practical, scalable system; human evaluation; clear methodology.  
**Weaknesses**: 2022 paper; 30-day training time is substantial; GuanDan-specific features may not transfer directly.  
**Reproducibility**: Low — no public code; methodology described but massive compute requirements.
