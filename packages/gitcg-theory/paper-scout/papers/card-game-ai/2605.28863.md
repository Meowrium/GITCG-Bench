# Self-Play Reinforcement Learning under Imperfect Information in Big 2
- **arXiv ID**: 2605.28863
- **Authors**: Aalok Patwa
- **Date**: 2026-05-21
- **Relevance Score**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

## Summary
This paper develops a self-play reinforcement learning framework for Big 2 (大老二), a 4-player imperfect-information card game popular in East Asia. It provides controlled comparisons between policy-gradient (PPO) and value-approximating agents (MC Q-approximation, SARSA, Q-learning). Key findings: PPO outperforms all value-based methods against random, greedy, and heuristic opponents; moderate entropy regularization prevents the policy from becoming overly deterministic; and current-policy self-play provides the strongest finite-budget training curriculum. The paper positions Big 2 as a useful testbed for studying deep RL under imperfect information, multiplayer interaction, delayed rewards, and variable action sets.

## Key Contributions
- First self-play RL framework specifically for Big 2 (4-player imperfect-information card game)
- Systematic comparison: PPO vs MC-Q vs SARSA vs Q-learning under identical environment, input representation, training budget, and evaluation protocol
- Demonstrates entropy regularization as critical for preventing over-deterministic play in card game policies
- Current-policy self-play outperforms checkpoint self-play and fixed-opponent training — practical guidance for training schedules
- Establishes Big 2 as a rigorous testbed for card game RL research

## Connection to Our Work
**Extremely high relevance to GI TCG AI pipeline.** Big 2 is structurally analogous to Genius Invokation TCG:
- **Imperfect information**: Both have hidden hands/decks, unknown opponent cards
- **Multiplayer**: 4-player Big 2 vs 2-player GI TCG (both feature interaction effects beyond 2p0s)
- **Variable action sets**: Big 2 has card-type-constrained legal moves; GI TCG has elemental/timing constraints
- **Sparse rewards**: Big 2 rewards at round-end; GI TCG rewards at game-end

The PPO dominance finding directly supports our PPO-based training approach. The entropy regularization insight is actionable — we should add entropy bonuses to prevent GI TCG agents from converging to a single "safe" strategy. The self-play curriculum finding (current-policy > checkpoint > fixed-opponent) guides our training schedule design.

Note: single-author paper — verify depth/rigor against other benchmarks. The Big 2 framework may serve as a simpler proxy testbed before scaling to GI TCG.

## Critical Assessment
**Strengths:**
- Clean experimental design with controlled comparisons
- Practical findings (entropy regularization, curriculum choice)
- Big 2 is an excellent middle-ground testbed between toy games (Kuhn/Leduc poker) and full-scale games

**Weaknesses:**
- Single author, no institutional affiliation listed — requires scrutiny of reproducibility
- No comparison against search-based methods (MCTS/ISMCTS)
- Relatively small scale — unclear if findings scale to GI TCG's complexity (elemental reactions, character switching, equipment)
- Not yet peer-reviewed (arXiv only)

**Reproducibility:** Unclear — no code repository mentioned, framework details limited
