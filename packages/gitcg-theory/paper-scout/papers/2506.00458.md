# Reinforcement Learning for Hanabi
- arXiv ID: 2506.00458
- Authors: Nina Cohen, Kordel K. France
- Date: 2025-05-31
- Relevance Score: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

## Summary
This paper systematically compares tabular and deep RL algorithms for Hanabi, a cooperative card game with incomplete information that has become a popular RL research benchmark. The study evaluates tabular Expected SARSA, deep Q-Network (DQN), and other temporal difference (TD) methods in both self-play and cross-agent matchups. Key findings: TD-based algorithms (Expected SARSA, DQN) showed best overall performance and balanced play types. Certain agents performed best against specific opponent types, indicating adaptive behavior. The work quantifies conditions under which each algorithm provides advantages.

## Key Contributions
- Comprehensive comparison of tabular vs. deep RL for cooperative imperfect-information card games
- Finding that Expected SARSA and DQN dominate among tested algorithms for Hanabi
- Cross-agent evaluation revealing adaptive behavior patterns
- Quantification of conditions favoring different algorithmic approaches
- Novel contribution: agents exhibit highest scores against specific opponents rather than universally

## Connection to Our Work
- **Hanabi as GI TCG proxy**: Hanabi's cooperative IIG structure differs from GI TCG's competitive 1v1, but the partial observability challenges (hidden cards) are analogous. Hanabi's information sharing mechanics are a simplified version of the inference problem in GI TCG.
- **TD methods validation**: The finding that TD-based methods outperform pure Monte Carlo approaches supports our use of value-based critics in GI TCG's MCTS+RL pipeline.
- **Cross-agent evaluation**: Their methodology of testing agents against diverse opponent types is directly applicable to GI TCG agent evaluation — we should test against diverse deck archetypes and playstyles.
- **Expected SARSA success**: The strong performance of Expected SARSA (which averages over action distributions) aligns with the idea that GI TCG agents should reason about expected opponent responses rather than single deterministic outcomes.

## Critical Assessment
**Strengths**: Systematic comparison with clear methodology. Practical insights about algorithm selection for card game domains. Cross-agent evaluation design is well-structured.

**Weaknesses**: Relatively simple tabular RL methods — doesn't explore modern deep RL architectures (PPO, SAC). Single-game focus (Hanabi). Limited novelty — primarily an empirical comparison rather than methodological innovation.

**Reproducibility**: Good — Hanabi is a standard OpenSpiel environment. Algorithm implementations are standard.
